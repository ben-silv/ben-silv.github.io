Hobbies

None of this is a transferable skill and I'm not going to pretend otherwise. It's just what I do when nobody's paying me.

Leatherworking

The one I'd talk about for an hour. Slow, forgiving of patience, completely unforgiving of rushing, and the only thing I do where a mistake is permanent the second it happens.

The same wallet, a year apart

I draw the patterns myself and prototype them in paper first, because leather doesn't forgive a measurement you guessed at. Most of what got better between those two wallets is that I stopped guessing.

Bikes, mostly in pieces

I ride mountain bikes, and the half I like best is the one where the bike is on the floor in pieces. Brakes bled and upgraded, a dropper post fitted, headset and gears dialled in, a new cassette — most of what's on mine now, I put there. Down to cable ends that match the frame, which nobody notices but me.

Also, with less to show for them

Gear trading

Marketplace, mostly, hunting parts for the bikes. I'll find the used one — half the price and usually in better shape than the listing admits.

Knife sharpening

Whetstones, a consistent angle, an edge that bites paper. The one I'm proudest of is my grandfather's deba, the knife he filleted fish with, brought back from blunt.

Taiwanese food

Danzai noodles first, Taiwanese pork chop rice a close second. I'll argue about both.

Model rocketry

The L1 certification rocket with Tufts SEDS — drawn, cut, glued and flown, which is the only part that counts.

Currently hunting for a used wheelset that isn't a scam and a shoulder of veg-tan I can justify.
