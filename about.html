About

I came to Tufts for computer science and added a focus in biological sciences once it turned out the biology was the part I couldn't stop reading about. Those two things looked unrelated for about a year and then stopped being unrelated at all.

I grew up in a Taiwanese-American household, which is where I learned that the answer to most questions is to take the lid off and look. That habit is more or less the whole story: a bike that shifts badly gets stripped to the frame, a knife that won't bite gets a whetstone and an argument about angles, and a dataset with a million immune cells in it gets a pipeline until the clusters mean something.

At Tufts Technology Services I look after tooling for research storage. The old request workflow had staff retyping the same details into a fixed format by hand; mine takes a paste, picks the template for the kind of request being handled, and formats it. About a quarter of the time back on each one, a single consistent format at the end of it, and nothing for a new starter to memorise.

Data is one big haystack and computer science is the magnet. That is most of why I like this: finding the pattern, and then getting it in front of somebody who can act on it. Biotech and healthcare data are where I would most like that to land, though the part I actually care about — prediction, patterns, making numbers usable — travels anywhere data does.
