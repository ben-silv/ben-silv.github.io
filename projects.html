02 — Projects

Two apps for bad days.

Both are health tools built for moments where the stakes are real and nobody has patience for a bad interface. One is live; the other taught me when to cut a feature.

CraveCast

A craving forecaster for addiction recovery. It predicts when an urge is likely to hit, warns you before it lands, and turns every day you stay clean into something growing in a garden. Built on the peer-reviewed ADARP study plus personalised kernel-density modelling.

No sign-up, no tracking — a single-file HTML app that works fully offline, so nothing about someone's recovery ever leaves their browser. That constraint drove most of the architecture.

Epialert

Allergen detection for travellers with severe dietary restrictions. Photograph a dish, get allergen detection and a risk-level warning, then a live map of the pharmacies and hospitals nearby that actually stock epinephrine auto-injectors.

I cut the peer-response feed late in development. Letting users vouch for a restaurant sounded good until I worked through what crowd-sourced safety data means when the failure mode is anaphylaxis.
